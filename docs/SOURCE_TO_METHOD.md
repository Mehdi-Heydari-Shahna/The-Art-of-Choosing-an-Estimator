# Source-to-method register

Each executable method is listed with its source, the mathematical statement used, the numerical adaptation made
for this project, what it needs and returns, how it was checked independently, and its tested limits.
"Method coverage" (which statements of a source are implemented) is summarized at the end.

| K1 | Time-varying Kalman filter with known inputs, measurement update then time update | R. E. Kalman, "A new approach to linear filtering and prediction problems," *Trans. ASME J. Basic Eng.* 82(1):35–45, 1960. Used: the optimal estimate under quadratic loss is the conditional expectation (Theorem 1 and Theorem 1-a of the paper, as numbered in the excerpts), which is linear in the data for Gaussian processes; without Gaussianity the same recursion gives the best linear estimate (orthogonal projection); recursive gain and covariance ("variance") equation. The paper's model has no separate measurement-noise term; the additive-noise form used here is the standard extension (noise appended to the state), as in R. E. Kalman and R. S. Bucy, "New results in linear filtering and prediction theory," *Trans. ASME J. Basic Eng.* 83(1):95–108, 1961 for continuous time. | Joseph-form covariance update and symmetrisation (legacy kernel `kalman_gains`, reused verbatim); Cholesky solve of the innovation covariance | A, B, C, Qd, R, x̂₀, P₀, y₀..y_N, u₀..u_{N−1} → x̂_{k\|k}, P_{k\|k}, x̂_{k+1\|k}, P_{k+1\|k}, gains, innovations, NIS | Batch conditioning of the stacked joint Gaussian in 40-digit arithmetic (`independent/ref_kalman.batch_filtered`): estimates agree to 2.2e-14 of the prior standard deviation and covariances to 6.5e-15 relative over 21 cases from 7 families (`results/fidelity.json`) | 1–4 states, 1–2 outputs, 30–300 steps; R ≻ 0 required; no cross-covariance, no coloured noise, no irregular sampling, no parameter uncertainty (each is refused with its reason; revision 2: parameter ranges in the second-order block are detected, and recorded timestamps are compared with the period) |
| K2 | Steady-state (constant-gain) filter | Stabilising solution of the filter Riccati equation (same sources; existence and independence of P₀ under detectability and stabilisability are classical results, e.g. as collected in B. D. O. Anderson and J. B. Moore, *Optimal Filtering*, Prentice-Hall, 1979) | `scipy.linalg.solve_discrete_are` on the dual pair; PBH rank tests (relative tolerance 1e-9) for detectability of (A, C) and stabilisability of (A, Qd^{1/2}) | A, C, Qd, R → M∞, P∞, P∞⁺ | Fixed point of the Riccati iteration from Qd + I (`riccati_fixed_point`): gains agree to 1.1e-9 relative | marginally stable modes excited by noise (double integrator, bias states) covered; modes on the unit circle not excited by Qd are refused. A steady-state statement refers to the limit k → ∞; with a deadline or a finite horizon the constant gain's error variance is evaluated from P₀ over the requested samples (P₀ is then required; review A, finding A3) |
| K3 | Sampling of a continuous model and of continuous process noise | Zero-order hold: matrix exponential of the augmented matrix. Noise: C. F. Van Loan, "Computing integrals involving the matrix exponential," *IEEE Trans. Autom. Control* 23(3):395–404, 1978. Not part of Kalman's papers. | legacy kernel `discretize_noise`, reused verbatim; the specification must state whether a noise value is a spectral density or a per-step covariance | Ac, Bc, G, Qc, Ts → Ad, Bd, Qd | Gauss–Legendre quadrature of matrix-exponential values (`zoh_quadrature`, `process_cov_quadrature`): 1.3e-15 relative | zero-order hold of the input only; one period for model, noise conversion and execution |
| K4 | Exact mean-square error of any constant or time-varying linear gain; probability statements | Covariance recursion of a fixed gain (affine in Q, R, P₀); Gaussian quantile; Chebyshev inequality when only second moments are assumed | discrete Lyapunov solve for constant gains | gain(s), model → error covariance; P(\|e\| ≤ r) level per time instant | Kronecker-product Lyapunov solution and Monte Carlo with Gaussian and uniform noise (`lyapunov_mse_of_gain`, `fixed_gain_mse_montecarlo`) | statements at finite times are the largest value over the requested samples, computed from P₀ (to the end of the record, or to convergence with the limit included when no end time is given; both covariance maps are monotone, which gives the stopping rule; reviews A2, A3, A7); probability statements are per time instant, not simultaneous over a window |

Claims issued: *conditional mean, minimum mean-square error among all estimators of the same data* only when the
specification states jointly Gaussian noises and initial error; otherwise *minimum mean-square error among linear
(affine) estimators*. A covariance is never presented as a deterministic bound.

Retained from the supplied package but outside the estimation register: the finite-horizon LQR recursion
(`riccati_lti`, from `kalman_agent/methods/lqr.py`), available as a controller for examples only. The supplied
package's completion and witness machinery for LQR weights is **not** part of this release; the defect found in it
(witnesses outside a declared interval) is reproduced in `regressions/legacy_defects.json`, and the new
coordinator's witnesses are checked for admissibility in `tests/test_regressions.py::test_K2`.

## Coordinator (`heros/coordinator/`)

Results of this project (elementary deductions, stated as such in the article): release under incomplete
information by monotonicity (exact envelope nondecreasing in V and D; fixed-gain covariance nondecreasing in the
noise covariances), disagreement witnesses restricted to the declared admissible set, harmless-omission arguments
(initial-error terms vanish in the ultimate bound; steady-state gain independent of P₀; the recursion is the same
for every distribution with the stated second moments).
